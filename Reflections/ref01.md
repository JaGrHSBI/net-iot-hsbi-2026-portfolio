## What was difficult?

I had trouble keeping up. Not for a specific task, but in general. I am personally not very well versed in git or in markdown, I am both able to find documentation and am able to adapt a given solution from the template to my use case, but that takes time. Time I did not have. It started with me not having the correct file open to follow the course, then I noticed that we were supposed to answer the questions from the presentation in our portfolio, but by that time the presentation was gone, so I had to go back and find it. Additionally we were trying to link each others git and trying to link to the PreStudy-screenshot, so we had to find out how this works as well, which resulted in us not even finishing task 1 before the break started (and we were supposed to start task 3 after the break). This led to us continously lagging behind and our adventure with trying to get a toggle to work inside NodeRed contributed to this further. I don't think it took us very long to complete each task, in fact I think we were pretty quick once we got it running, but we just couldn't keep up and I believe a major reason for this is that the structure of the module is lacking.  
I'm going to hone in on this for now. In the middle of "Opening the Class" we are getting the first direction on what to add to the portfolio. "Who I am" had already happened at that point, without any reference to this belonging in the portfolio. "My pod" is both a description on what to write in this section and also a description on how to work with the portfolio later on. Why is this not separated? The pre-study artefact relates to what happens after this section, the study log to something that happens even later, after the unnecessary story-insert, so is even further removed and then we get the optional story seeds that relates to the story-insert that in this module sits before the study-task and has not even been established by this point at all.  
But now is where it actually starts breaking: The Pod Study describes something to do and it involves quite a lot of text/space, it's likely going to take up two full screens at least. AFTER THIS we get to Task 1 and Task 2 which describe what you're actually supposed to be with the Pod Study. So, the approach we're supposed to take here is apparently this: Read through everything once in case there is going to be a clarification later on how to do it. We see the same problem later on when at the end of the module we're presented with how we're supposed to prove our attainment of the learning goals (that were established at the top) and we see the same problem for every single task, only worse. The tasks refer to phases in the workbook - a different document - but the recommended portfolio evidence for completing that task is on the first document. We are working with laptops here, we only have one screen. Two different documents, a new program we are meant to use (for the first time in my case) and then a documentation we are supposed to write is a lot to juggle. We were constantly trying to switch windows and tabs to find what we were looking for, this disrupts the workflow and wastes time that we seem to have very little of. This adds a lot of unnecessary cognitive load (there is a name for this in CLT, split attention effect). And on the topic of cognitive load: The story hooks are a distraction. They are not fulfilling the role of a story hook, since they don't actually motivate the task or explain the task and the task does not originate from the story organically. They do not serve a purpose, they are redundant, they increase cognitive load (also has a name in CLT, fittingly: redundancy-effect).  
  
I am new to this topic as a whole. I have no experience with networks or IoT at all, I only understood how IP-addresses (v4 apparently) were set up during the PreStudy and I barely know what MQTT even is. The tasks were not difficult, they appeared to me very achievable, the tasks were not the problem. The presentation was.  
I believe the fact that there we so many questions in class relating to how we were supposed to present our documentation in the portfolio is a testament to this.  
I admit I was rambling a bit here, but to be honest I was really frustrated after this first session.
